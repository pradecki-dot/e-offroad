How to choose your first electric quad — a guide

e-offroad.com · 15 November 2026

You usually buy a first electric quad once every few years, so it’s worth starting with the question of what it will really be used for, and only then looking at power and price.

What will the quad be used for?

For leisure rides around a meadow, a lighter model with simple suspension is enough. If the quad is to carry firewood, feed or tools, payload, racks and a tow hitch matter. For sport riding you need long suspension travel and adjustable damping.

1000 W or 2000 W?

A 1000 W electric quad is enough for a rider up to about 70 kg on flat ground. A 2000 W electric quad has torque in reserve for climbs, wet grass and towing a trailer, and in practice the motor and controller run cooler during long work sessions.

Battery and charging

Compare energy in watt-hours (voltage × capacity), not just amps. A lithium battery is lighter and lasts longer than a lead-acid one. Check whether the charger is included and how long a full charge takes.

Suspension and brakes

Double wishbones at the front give better wheel contact with the ground than a rigid axle. Disc brakes on both axles are standard today and not something to economise on.

What to watch out for when buying

Ask about parts availability, the battery warranty terms and who will carry out servicing. The cheapest quad from an auction site with no seller support soon turns out to be the most expensive.
