Riding an electric motorcycle legally in the forest

e-offroad.com · 15 November 2026

A quiet motor tempts you to turn onto a forest path. The law makes no exception for electric drive, though, and fines and conflicts with foresters can easily spoil a season.

What the Polish Forest Act says

Under Article 29 of the Polish Forest Act, motor vehicles may only travel in forests on public roads, and on forest roads only where signs permit it. Electric drive changes nothing here. This applies to forests regardless of ownership, and entering without permission can result in a fine.

Does electric drive change anything?

No. Electric motorcycles and quads are motor vehicles under the law, just like their petrol equivalents. Being quiet and emission-free is no grounds for entry. The situation is different for an electric bike with pedal assistance up to 25 km/h and a motor of up to 250 W — the law treats it as a bicycle, but it’s still worth checking local rules for cycling in the forest with the forest district office.

Where to ride legally

The safest options are motocross and enduro tracks, off-road parks, organised events and private land with the owner’s written permission. Some forest districts mark out routes or open land for competitions — always ask locally.

Public roads and registration

Only a type-approved and registered vehicle with valid third-party insurance, ridden by a person with the appropriate licence, may use public roads. Most off-road equipment, including kids’ motorcycles, has no such documents. This text is not legal advice — check the current regulations before riding.
